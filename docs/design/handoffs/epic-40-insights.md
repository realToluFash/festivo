## What this area is

Not a dashboard for its own sake: the answers to questions an organiser actually asks. Are
replies coming in faster than last time? Which channel works? Who has not answered?

## Candidate artboards

- **Insights** — the reports

## Layouts, and why

**Desktop mostly**, with headline numbers worth having in the apps. Printable, because this
is what gets taken into a conversation with whoever is paying.

## What these screens must honour

- **Every figure leads somewhere.** "Eleven bounced" is a list of eleven people to fix, not
  a statistic. A number that cannot be acted on is a statistic.
- A range is shown as a range. A single confident figure where a range exists is a lie
  somebody will plan against.
- Honest about sample size. One event is not a trend.
- Charts work in black and white and for a colour-blind reader: categorical palettes differ
  in lightness, not hue alone.

## Traps specific to this epic

- **It reads cost figures from Budget rather than computing them** (sc-106). A reporting
  screen doing its own arithmetic produces a second answer, and the one on the report is
  the one somebody quotes in a meeting.
- Cost figures need the budget permission; margin needs its own. A reporting surface is the
  most likely place for a permission to be forgotten.
- Percentages are of the whole room, never of replies received.
