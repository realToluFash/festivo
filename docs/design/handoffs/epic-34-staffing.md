## What this area is

Who works the event. Deliberately not the same as being in the organisation: a waiter
hired for Saturday is not in your company. Shifts, stations, and what each person is
responsible for.

## Candidate artboards

- **Staffing** — assigning people, shifts and stations

## Layouts, and why

**All four.** Arranged on a computer, used from the apps — and the person using it may be
on a borrowed device.

## What these screens must honour

- They get in **without necessarily having an account**, bounded by the event and gone
  afterwards.
- On the night, **granting access takes seconds**. Somebody standing in a doorway cannot
  wait for an invitation email.
- A staff member sees **only their own job** — their tables, their shift, their station.
- A shift carries a start and an end, so hours can be counted (sc-101).

## Traps specific to this epic

- These are the people **least likely to have used the product before**, being onboarded
  at the worst possible moment: loud, dim, busy, already behind. Whatever they see must be
  self-explanatory on first contact.
- A role may carry a rate, visible only with the budget permission. A chef and a waiter do
  not cost the same.
- Reassigning somebody mid-service is a real flow, not an edge case.
